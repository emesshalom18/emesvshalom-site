The Brodericks

The dispute with R' Ranaan and Mrs. Soro Rochel Broderick: custodial accounts, real estate, and the Queens lawsuit.

Who they are in this story

Mrs. Soro Rochel (Sarah) Broderick, a married daughter of R’ Avrohom Ort, and her husband R’ Ranaan Broderick. Correspondence signed “the true friends of the Brodericks” lays out three financial issues and a parallel dispute over whether the family would submit to a Din Torah.

The three financial issues, as the correspondence presents them

  1. Custodial accounts. Over $180,000 in cash that Mrs. Broderick acquired by taking ownership of custodial accounts her father had established in her name. A Salomon Smith Barney letter of September 2002, confirming the account history going back to December 1999, is cataloged: catalog entry — view the letter .

  2. Real estate that R’ Avrohom inherited from his parents and placed in her name as a legal tax shelter, which she assumed control of.

  3. Substantial income generated by the above.

The correspondence notes that R’ Avrohom only learned of the December 1999 account changes in September 2002 — which, it says, is why he gave his daughter $2,500 in April 2000 when she told him she had no money.

The Queens lawsuit

Mrs. Broderick and her sister Mrs. Chana Miriam Hirsch sued their father (and Philip Ort) in New York Supreme Court, Queens County (Index No. 5637/03), over income from the real estate — a claim the correspondence puts at one million dollars plus interest and legal fees:

  • Summons and Complaint cover — view .
  • Third Count: injunction against the rabbinic arbitration — view .
  • Affirmation opposing the third motion to restore the action after its May 2006 dismissal — view .

The rabbinic response, as the correspondence presents it

  • Rav Nota Greenblatt’s February 2003 letter denouncing the “secret heter” — the claim, attributed to Mr. Broderick, that a secret rov had permitted suing in civil court, for a secret reason: catalog entry — view .
  • Rav Greenblatt’s third hazmana to Mrs. Broderick (May 2013): catalog entry .
  • A joint rabbinical letter on the Din Torah summons (April 2014): catalog entry .

Mrs. Broderick’s replies

The correspondence also publishes Mrs. Broderick’s side — her April 2013 letter to Rabbi Fried rebutting her father’s letter point by point (disputing the characterization of R’ Yitzchak Abadi’s institutions and donations, the claim about Rav Hershel Shechter as dayan, and Rav Greenblatt’s account of their discussions): catalog entry — view .

The open responses answer each of her points in turn — on the documented donations to R’ Abadi’s institutions, on what disqualifies a dayan, and on the sequence of the siruv versus the arbitration agreement.

This page summarizes the Broderick correspondence. Allegations about theft, motives, and the validity of heterim are the correspondence’s characterizations, not findings of the archive; the documents linked above are presented as cataloged.
