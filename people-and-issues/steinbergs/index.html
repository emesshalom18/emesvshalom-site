People and issues

The Steinbergs

The correspondence with Rabbi Yisroel and Mrs. Miriam Steinberg of Lakewood.

Background

Rabbi Yisroel Steinberg and Mrs. Miriam Steinberg of Lakewood — Mrs. Steinberg is described in the correspondence as Mrs. Manya Ort’s friend since kindergarten. A series of open letters addressed to the couple argues that they publicly portrayed Mrs. Ort as “penniless” and “starving,” and urges them to call on her to bring the dispute to a Beis Din.

The $3,000 chesed loan

According to the letters, Rabbi Steinberg — then president of the g’mach of Cong. Zichron Shneur — arranged a $3,000 “chesed loan” for Mrs. Ort. The letters quote his July 2002 deposition: “she said she needed $3,000 and… I wrote out the check.”

The deposition transcript is cataloged in the archive: catalog entry — view the transcript .

The letters’ counter-argument

The open letters argue the “penniless” characterization cannot stand against the documents:

  • Mrs. Ort’s handwritten authorization (January 2000) to wire $350,000 to Israel, and her deposition confirming the combined $600,000 — catalog entry .
  • The court order directing $25,000 per month in child support, referenced in the letters.
  • The rabbinic letters calling for a Din Torah in Beis Din only — the five-signature letter of Rav Moshe Sternbuch and fellow Gedolei HaDor ( catalog entry ), and the letters of Rav Dovid Feinstein and Rav Nota Greenblatt ( catalog entry , catalog entry ).
  • Rosh Yeshiva Harav Malkiel Kotler’s letter to the judge stating the dispute “could and should be resolved in a bais din,” and the appearance of Harav Dovid Shustal and Harav Yisroel Neuman at the Ocean County courthouse — presented as Lakewood’s own Roshei Yeshiva taking the position that the matter belongs in Beis Din.

Each letter closes the same way: an invitation to send a statement or any supporting facts for publication, and the argument that declining to do so speaks for itself.

The February 2012 incident

The correspondence reports that on February 10, 2012, after one of the letters was sent, Mrs. Steinberg called the police to arrest a man holding a sign publicizing Rabbi Ort’s position. An attorney’s letter about the detention is cataloged: catalog entry — view the letter .

The letters present this as an attempt to keep the documents from the public, and followed it with further open letters repeating the call to acknowledge the documentation and urge Mrs. Ort toward a Din Torah.
