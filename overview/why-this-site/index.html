Overview

Why this site exists

The purpose of this documentation site, adapted from the original.

This site was created to provide information. It has been said that because R’ Avraham Ort left his family penniless, Mrs. Ort was forced to turn to the secular courts. This site presents the other side of the story — documentation that, according to its authors, shows Mrs. Ort received millions of dollars in liquid assets over the years, in addition to real estate, while R’ Avraham was left with the child-support obligations.

The goal

The authors’ stated hope is that readers, seeing the documentation, will help bring the parties to resolve their differences in a Beis Din, as halacha requires — the Roshei Yeshiva of Beth Medrash Govoha have repeatedly called for the monetary disputes to be adjudicated before a Beis Din rather than in secular court. Several rabbinical rulings on this point are collected under Rabbonim Letters.

The site states explicitly that it does not claim to know who is right and to what extent — only that there is a proper forum for resolving such disputes, and the matter belongs there.

What is not the goal

The purpose of this site and its documentation is not to attack anyone personally. As the original site put it: in any divorce, emotions run high and people act under duress in ways they otherwise would not. What matters is not the past but attempting healing for the future.

R’ Avraham has stated his willingness to adjudicate the matter before the Beis Din selected by the Roshei Yeshiva as impartial, or to work out a settlement providing for Mrs. Ort and the children.

What is presented here

  • A brief history of the case.
  • A financial overview summarizing the documented transfers, support payments, and property transactions — totaling $11,175,850 across the categories the site tracks — with links to the underlying evidence.
  • The evidence archive: scans of checks, letters, depositions, and property documents, each cataloged with its source, date, and a quality label.
  • Background on the people and issues involved, including the rabbinical letters concerning the case.
