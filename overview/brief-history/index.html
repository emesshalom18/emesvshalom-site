A brief history of the Ort case

A short timeline of the case, adapted from the original site.

Mrs. Ort filed for divorce in New Jersey state court. According to the authors of this site, she did so without the permission of a Beis Din; R’ Avraham was not consulted by the rav she spoke to regarding the monetary issues. A letter signed by three Lakewood Roshei Yeshiva and Rav Dovid Feinstein did not reverse the filing.

June 2000. R’ Avraham moved back into the family home to attempt a reconciliation, remaining for fourteen months. During this time he consented to the release of over $300,000 from the couple’s joint account. All accounts had been frozen at filing, as is usual, to prevent dissipation of assets before settlement.

August 2001. Reconciliation having failed, he left the home again. Mrs. Ort returned to court and was awarded pendente lite support of $25,000 per month, drawn from the couple’s joint account, which held $700,000 when the case opened.

2003. The joint account ran dry. The court then permitted Mrs. Ort to draw support from the children’s accounts — funds from R’ Avraham’s family that had been disbursed among the children’s accounts, totaling over one million dollars. Because the money now came from the children’s accounts, the court lowered the monthly amount to $11,000. These withdrawals continued until 2016, when the last child was emancipated.

2007. The court case concluded. Rav Dovid Schustal and Rav Yisroel Neumann came to court to support R’ Avraham; Rav Malkiel Kotler wrote the judge a letter of support. The final settlement awarded Mrs. Ort the remaining marital assets — the joint account having been spent down on support — and residential custody of the children, which R’ Avraham agreed to for the children’s stability.

Throughout, the Roshei Yeshiva pressed for the monetary disputes to be heard by a Beis Din. In 2002 the Baltimore Bais Din issued a ksav siruv regarding Mrs. Ort’s refusal to appear for a din Torah; in 2008 it issued an ikul restraining the sale of the Lakewood house pending a din Torah. The letters are collected under Rabbonim Letters.

The children. R’ Avraham and Mrs. Ort were awarded joint legal custody; he agreed to give her full residential custody to minimize disruption. The site’s authors write that the children have been turned against their father by the belief that he left their mother impoverished, and that resolving the financial questions in a Beis Din is the path to rebuilding the relationship.
