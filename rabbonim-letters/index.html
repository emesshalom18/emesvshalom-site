Rabbonim Letters

Rabbinical rulings and writs concerning the case, with summaries and scans.

The original site collected fourteen letter scans from the rabbonim involved with the case over the years. The pilot catalogs all fourteen below, in the original page’s groupings: the writs concerning Mrs. Ort, the letters of the Roshei Yeshiva, and related correspondence. Each entry links to its full catalog record (with transcription notes and quality labels) in the Evidence Archive .

Writs concerning Mrs. Ort

Ksiv siruv — Baltimore Bais Din (June 2002)

A Hebrew writ of refusal (ksav siruv) concerning Mrs. Manya Ort of Lakewood, NJ: she refused to appear for a din Torah regarding the monetary claims between her and her husband, and instead pursued them in secular courts without permission. Dated in the document to Isru Chag Shavuos 5762. Signed by three dayanim, with the bais din’s seal.

Bais Din ruling on mesira (November 19, 2002)

A ruling from Rabbi Mordechai Shuchatowitz, for the dayanim of the Baltimore Bais Din, concerning Mrs. Ort’s “Equitable Distribution” claim in secular court: beyond the prohibition of going to secular courts, the ruling states the graver prohibition of mesira was committed, citing “criminal allegations” said to have been made. Dated in the document Tuesday, 14 Kislev 5763. Signed by three dayanim.

Siruv regarding Mrs. Chana Miriam Hirsch (January 16, 2003)

A Hebrew notice from the Baltimore Bais Din concerning Mrs. Chana Miriam, wife of R’ Eliezer Chaim Hirsch of Monsey NY (a daughter of R’ Avraham Ort), for proceeding in secular courts without beis din permission. Dated in the document Thursday, 13 Shevat 5763. Three signatures; the bais din seal is affixed.

Ikul — Baltimore Bais Din (August 3, 2008)

A beis din restraining order (ikul) concerning Mrs. Manya Ort of 1015 Forest Ave., Lakewood NJ, restraining the sale of the Lakewood house until she appears for a din Torah, and warning all parties that assisting the sale violates the order. Signed by Rav Moshe Heinemann, Rav Yakov Hopper, and Rav Mordechai Shuchatowitz, with the bais din’s seal. Dated 2 Menachem-Av 5768.

She’elah to Rav Nissim Karelitz (October 12, 2008)

Rabbi Mordechai Shuchatowitz of Baltimore writes to Rav Nissim Karelitz asking whether one may rely on a psak permitting recourse to secular courts without beis din leave in the matter of Mrs. Manya Ort vs. R’ Avraham Ort — or whether a heter requires hearing both sides and summoning the other party first. Hebrew, with key passages underlined. Dated 13 Tishrei 5769.

Rabbi Malkiel Kotler to Judge Arnold Goodman (April 16, 2008)

A typed English letter from Rabbi Malkiel Kotler, Dean of Beth Medrash Govoha (Lakewood NJ), to Judge Arnold Goodman. Rabbi Kotler writes that he has known Rabbi Abraham Ort for many years as “a very honest and decent person”; that the court should take Ort’s claim of inability to pay the outstanding child support at face value and defer the obligations rather than enforce the judgment, lest it “run the risk of incarcerating an innocent man”; and that “this entire dispute could and should be resolved in a Bais Din” — a position he says he has communicated to Mrs. Ort as well.

Related correspondence

Joint rabbinical letter on the Broderick din Torah summons (April 2014)

Dated Rosh Chodesh Nissan 5774. The letter’s English section addresses “the painful matter of the complaint and claims on Mrs. Soro Rochel Broderick, wife of Rav Ranaan Broderick”: summoned to a din Torah by her father Rav Avrohom Ort over “an enormous amount of money,” she refuses adjudication in a bais din whose arbitrator has no connection to the litigation between her father and mother. The letter calls on the community, the rabbonim, and anyone of influence to press her to accept bais din authority. Signed by (Horav) Nota Tzvi Greenblat and joined by (Horav) Shlomo Eliyahu Miller, (Horav) Yaakov Perlow, (Horav) Arye Malkiel Kotler, (Horav) Yisroel Tzvi Neuman, (Horav) Aharon Feldman, and (Horav) Aharon Moshe Shechter; the Hebrew section carries additional signatures.
